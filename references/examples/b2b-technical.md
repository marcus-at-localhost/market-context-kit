# Example Pack: B2B Technical, Industrial & Regulated

Load this pack only when `references/business-context.md` resolves the business to an industrial manufacturer, technical supplier or distributor, regulated/compliance-driven business, training academy, knowledge-led B2B brand, or enterprise sales-led software.

Assumptions behind everything here: a buying cycle measured in months, multiple people in the decision (engineer, procurement, quality/compliance, management), a commercial action that is a request rather than a purchase, budget that is not the buyer's own money, and a buyer who is personally accountable if the choice fails in service.

Consequences that shape every example below:

- **Nobody signs up.** They request a quote, a datasheet, a sample, a technical review, a site visit, or a course seat. The conversion is a qualified conversation.
- **Risk reversal is proof, not refunds.** Certifications, approvals, test data, reference installations, named experts, response-time commitments. A money-back guarantee is meaningless when the cost of failure is unplanned downtime.
- **Urgency is real or absent.** Countdown timers and "12 spots left" destroy credibility with a procurement buyer. Real urgency is lead time, stock, a standard's transition deadline, an audit date, or a course calendar.
- **The buyer's own vocabulary is the copy.** Technical precision reads as competence. Simplifying a material designation or a standard number is not clarity, it is a warning sign.

Examples are written in English. Render CTAs and headlines in the site's language using the terminology from `_grounding/`, not by translating these strings literally.

## Buyer archetypes

McKinsey's B2B Pulse Survey (2024, ~4,000 decision makers) finds three recurring buyer types. Figures are global-survey directional, not this client's actual mix — grounding buyer research overrides if present.

| Archetype | Share | Behavior | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Adapter** | 44% | Relationship-oriented, sticks with familiar channels and suppliers even when unsatisfied, slow to try new ones | Weight toward the human-default items in `Channels` — trade fairs, named rep, association contact. Don't force self-serve as the only path. |
| **Innovator** | 20% | Digital-first, comfortable using gen-AI tools to research suppliers, wants self-serve and technical depth | Prioritize selector/calculator tools and lead magnets from `Funnel — friction causes and fixes`, structured technical content from `Hooks and post openers`, ungated datasheets. |
| **Seeker** | 36% | Demands a seamless omnichannel experience; switches supplier fast on a broken touchpoint | Check every commitment level in `CTA phrasing` actually works — this archetype punishes the weakest link, not the average. |

All three coexist on any site; none is safe to ignore. If grounding names a dominant buyer type, or site evidence points to one (a distributor-heavy funnel skews Adapter, a self-serve configurator skews Innovator), lead with that implication — but keep the other two paths open.

## 1. Channels

| Channel | Role | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn (company + expert personal profiles) | Authority, hiring, reaching specifiers | 2-3x/week |
| Trade press and industry portals | Reach buyers who never search generically | Per editorial calendar |
| Technical newsletter | Owned channel, longest-lived asset in B2B | Monthly or quarterly |
| Webinars and technical talks | Qualified lead capture with real intent | 4-8x/year |
| Industry associations and standards bodies | Credibility no ad buy can produce | Ongoing membership |
| Trade fairs | Still the highest-value touchpoint in most industrial sectors | 1-4x/year |
| YouTube (technical) | Installation, failure analysis, application demos | Monthly |
| Distributor and partner channels | Their reach, your content | Ongoing enablement |

Consumer-social presence is optional and usually a recruiting or employer-branding play, not a demand channel. Do not recommend TikTok, Reels, or hashtag strategies here unless the brand already runs them successfully.

## 2. Hooks and post openers

Authority comes from specificity and from being willing to say something a generalist cannot.

```
"[Standard] changes in [year]. What it means for [application]:"
"We cut open a failed [component] after [X] months in service. Here's what we found:"
"Why [common material] fails at [condition] — and what we specify instead"
"[Application] at [temperature]/[pressure]/[medium]: the three specs that actually decide it"
"A customer asked whether [assumption] is true. We tested it."
"The most common specification error we see in [industry] RFQs:"
"[Regulation] compliance in practice: what auditors actually ask for"
"From our lab: [measurement] across [X] samples of [material]"
"What we learned at [trade fair]: [X] things buyers in [industry] are asking about"
```

Notice what is absent: no "unpopular opinion", no personal-transformation arc, no "secrets they don't want you to know". Conspiracy framing and hot takes cost credibility with a technical audience.

## 3. Distribution instead of hashtags

Hashtag tiering is not a B2B mechanism. Reach comes from:

- **Named topics and terminology** — write the standard number, the material designation, the application. Search and LinkedIn topic matching both key on those.
- **Expert profiles** — a post from a named engineer outperforms the company page. Build 2-3 expert profiles deliberately.
- **Trade media placement** — one article in the right industry publication outperforms a month of posting.
- **Association channels** — member newsletters, working groups, event programmes.
- **Distributor and partner amplification** — supply them ready-made content.
- **1-3 topic hashtags on LinkedIn only**, matching the industry's actual usage.

## 4. Engagement tactics

```
Technical question:  "How do you currently document [requirement] for [audit]?"
Specification poll:  "Which factor decides your [component] choice: temperature, media,
                      approval, or lead time?"
Standards commentary: neutral explanation of a revision, plus practical implication
Application case:    problem, constraint, what was specified, measured result
Ask-the-expert:      collect questions, answer them in a monthly post or webinar
Trade-fair recap:    what buyers asked about, what changed in the market
```

Avoid debate-bait ("change my mind") and founder-journey storytelling. The equivalent trust move is showing your work: data, method, caveats.

## 5. Repurposing chain

One substantial technical asset becomes:

```
SOURCE: whitepaper, test report, application study, or standards guide

 1. Webinar or technical talk built on the same material
 2. Trade-press article (adapted, editorial voice, no sales copy)
 3. LinkedIn post series from the expert's profile — one finding per post
 4. Technical newsletter feature
 5. Product/solution page section citing the finding
 6. Datasheet or selection-guide update
 7. Sales enablement one-pager for the field team and distributors
 8. Conference or association talk proposal
 9. FAQ entries answering the questions the webinar surfaced
10. YouTube explainer for the single most-asked question
```

Schedule over 6-12 weeks, not 2. B2B assets have a long half-life — the same whitepaper can be re-promoted for two years.

## 6. Landing pages — objections and how to answer them

| Objection | How to address |
| --- | --- |
| "Will this meet our standard / pass our audit?" | Named certifications, approvals, test reports, audit documentation |
| "Can it handle our application?" | Operating envelope (temperature, pressure, media, cycles), application matrix, expert consultation |
| "How fast can we get it?" | Lead time, stock levels, delivery commitment, distributor network, location |
| "Is this compatible with what we run?" | Cross-reference tables, dimensional data, replacement guides |
| "Can you prove it works in our industry?" | Reference installations, case studies with measured results, named customers where permitted |
| "What if it fails in service?" | Failure analysis support, warranty terms, technical support commitment, service network |
| "Who is behind this technically?" | Named experts with credentials, lab capability, engineering depth |
| "Can you supply at our volume, continuously?" | Capacity, production sites, quality system, second-source strategy |
| "Procurement needs alternatives" | Comparison against alternatives on technical grounds, total cost of ownership |

**Risk reducers that work here:** certification and approval proof, test data, sample dispatch, first-article inspection, named technical contact, guaranteed response time, reference customer call, on-site or remote technical review, pilot or trial installation.

## 7. CTA phrasing

Order by commitment, and offer more than one level on every page:

| Level | Intent | English pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Take the evidence away | "Download the datasheet" / "Get the selection guide" |
| Low | Self-qualify | "Check compatibility" / "Use the selector" |
| Medium | Human contact, no obligation | "Request a technical review" / "Talk to an engineer" |
| Medium | Physical proof | "Request a sample" |
| High | Commercial | "Request a quote" / "Send your specification" |
| High | Scheduled | "Reserve a course seat" / "Book a site visit" |

Rules: never gate the datasheet behind an account, state what happens after submission and by when, put the certification and expert proof next to the form, and always offer a direct phone or email route for urgent buyers.

Do **not** apply consumer CTA conventions here: no first-person button text, no colour psychology, no fake scarcity, no "no credit card required".

## 8. Page copy structures

**Industrial product / product category page:**

1. Technical descriptor plus application context
2. Qualification statement: material, standards, temperature/pressure/media range, industries
3. Use cases — including where the product should **not** be specified
4. Specifications: scannable table, datasheet link, standards and certifications, compatibility
5. Proof: test data, approvals, case examples, expert attribution
6. CTA: datasheet download, sample request, or technical consultation

**Industry / solution page:**

1. Name the industry and the operational problem
2. Stakes: downtime cost, compliance risk, safety, audit failure, cost of inaction
3. Solution architecture: which products and services combine to solve the application
4. Proof: standards, certifications, case study, named expert, process
5. Conversion block: expert contact, RFQ, datasheet, checklist, or assessment

**Technical service / academy page:**

1. Outcome: what the buyer will be able to do afterwards
2. Credentials: instructor or expert authority, certifications, partner bodies
3. Format and process: duration, location, schedule, deliverables, prerequisites
4. Proof: past participants, customer examples, audit and compliance relevance
5. CTA: reserve a seat, request a date, or speak to the trainer

**Compliance / regulatory page:**

1. Name the regulation or standard explicitly
2. Practical implication: what the buyer must decide, document, or change
3. Technical interpretation: clear guidance with caveats and evidence
4. The company's role, without unsupported legal claims
5. CTA: checklist, consultation, technical assessment, documentation download

**Distributor / catalog page:**

1. Findability first: search, filters, part numbers, cross-references
2. Availability: stock, lead time, minimum order, packaging units
3. Technical data at the item level, downloadable
4. Reorder and portal paths for existing customers
5. CTA: add to quote request, check availability, or contact the branch

## 9. Funnel — friction causes and fixes

**RFQ / inquiry forms:**

| Cause | Signal | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form demands commercial data too early | Company size, budget, purchase timing required | Ask for the technical problem first; qualify later |
| No file upload | Buyer cannot attach a drawing or spec | Add upload for drawings, specs, photos of the failed part |
| Silence about what happens next | No response-time statement | "You will hear from an engineer within [X] working days" |
| Proof missing at the form | Certifications elsewhere on the site | Put approvals and named expert beside the form |
| One CTA only | Only "contact us" | Offer datasheet, sample, and review as lower steps |
| Generic routing | Single inbox | Route by industry or application to the right specialist |

**Catalog / product findability:**

| Cause | Signal | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No part-number search | Search returns marketing pages | Index part numbers and competitor cross-references |
| No availability signal | Stock and lead time absent | Show both, even approximately |
| Datasheet gated | Registration required | Ungate; the datasheet is the qualification tool |
| No selection aid | Large range, no filters | Selector by application, medium, temperature, standard |

**Lead magnets, ranked for this audience:** datasheets and technical documentation; selection tools, calculators and configurators; standards and compliance checklists; test reports and application studies; failure-analysis guides; reference case studies with measured results; webinars and training previews; sample kits.

**RFQ / inquiry / enrollment page checklist:**

- [ ] Primary action matches buyer intent and names it concretely
- [ ] The page states what happens after submission, and when
- [ ] Required fields minimal; technical fields and file upload optional
- [ ] Certifications, standards, customer proof and expert credentials near the form
- [ ] Datasheets, course details or proof assets visible before any gate
- [ ] Direct phone and email route for urgent or high-intent buyers
- [ ] Language and units match the buyer's market

**Lifecycle → sequence:**

```
Anonymous visitor        → retargeting on technical content, not offers
Datasheet download       → technical follow-up, then sales handoff
Selector / calculator use → application-fit follow-up
Sample request           → dispatch confirmation, then application check-in
RFQ submitted            → response commitment, then quote follow-up
Course lead              → schedule reminder + proof sequence
Existing customer        → reorder, cross-range, standards updates
Dormant account          → new approvals, new range, or standards change as the reason to talk
```

## 10. Email — worked CTAs and framings

Problem-Agitate-Solution close, B2B form: _"Send us your specification and an engineer will come back within two working days."_

Sequence CTAs by stage: "Download the application guide", "Request a technical review", "Ask for a sample", "Reserve a seat on the next course", "Send your drawing for assessment".

Subject lines that work: _"[Standard] revision: what changes for [application]"_, _"Test results: [material] at [condition]"_, _"Your [component] question, answered"_, _"[Trade fair]: meet the team at stand [X]"_.

Real urgency only: course dates, standards transition deadlines, trade-fair timing, lead-time windows. Never invent a countdown.

**A low-frequency knowledge mail is a complete answer, not a partial one.** The owned email channel in this sector is usually a monthly or quarterly technical newsletter — knowledge, news and application notes to an audience that already knows the sender. Buying is paced by plant shutdowns, standards deadlines and tender cycles, not by send cadence, and the recipients are specifiers and maintenance leads for whom a weekly nurture track reads as noise from a supplier who does not understand their week.

Score and advise it against that purpose:

- Hitting a stated quarterly cadence is full marks. Missing cadence is the defect; low frequency is not.
- Lifecycle coverage is measured against the `Lifecycle → sequence` table above — the triggers that exist in this business. Absence of a multi-step behavioural drip is not a finding here.
- Segmentation that matters is by industry, application, material, standard, market language or account role — not by engagement score.
- Real lifecycle openings this sector does have and often misses: qualification or certificate expiry with a fixed date, standards revisions affecting an installed base, approval or material changes on a previously supplied part, and shutdown-season lead times. These are service and compliance touchpoints with a hard date attached, and they are worth naming even where a general nurture track would not be.

Do not recommend increasing frequency, lead scoring, or behavioural automation for this channel unless the client has stated a goal that requires them.

## 11. Ads — retargeting creative

```
Stage 1 (Awareness):
  1A Technical education — "[Standard/failure mode] explained for [industry]"
  1B Application problem — "[Component] failing at [condition]? Here's why."
  1C Authority — "[X] years supplying [industry]. [Certification] approved."

Stage 2 (Consideration):
  2A Case study — "How [customer type] eliminated [failure] in [application]"
  2B Technical comparison — "[Material A] vs [Material B] at [condition]"
  2C Documentation — "Selection guide for [application]: free download"
  2D Capability — "In-house testing to [standard]. See the lab."

Stage 3 (Conversion):
  3A RFQ — "Send your specification — technical response in [timeframe]"
  3B Compliance — "[Standard] documentation available for [application]"
  3C Sample — "Request a sample for your application test"
  3D Availability — "[Component] in stock, [lead time] delivery"
```

Budget reality: search and LinkedIn carry industrial demand; Meta and TikTok rarely do. Trade media and directories are priced per insertion or per period, not per click, and cost per lead runs far above consumer benchmarks while lead value runs far above them too.

## 12. Launch — industrial / technical timeline

Product Hunt, Reddit and "building in public" have no role here. A technical launch is a coordinated release of evidence, availability, and sales capability.

**Weeks 1-3 — Evidence and qualification**

- Complete test data, approvals and certification documentation
- Write the datasheet and specification sheet in every market language
- Define the operating envelope and the explicit non-applications
- Brief the technical support team; agree the response-time commitment

**Weeks 4-6 — Sales and channel enablement**

- Sales one-pager: application, differentiation, competitive alternatives, objection answers
- Distributor and partner briefing pack, plus co-branded assets
- Pricing, lead time, minimum order, sample policy confirmed
- Internal training session for field sales and inside sales

**Weeks 7-9 — Market preparation**

- Product, solution and category pages live with full technical data
- Trade-press pitch and editorial placement (long lead times — start here or earlier)
- Webinar or technical talk scheduled and promoted
- Trade-fair presence planned if the calendar allows
- Reference customer or pilot installation secured, permission to name agreed

**Weeks 10-12 — Launch and follow-through**

- Publish: pages, datasheets, application guide, announcement
- Expert-profile LinkedIn series, one finding per post
- Technical newsletter feature
- Distributor announcement and stock availability confirmed
- Follow up every inquiry within the committed response time
- Post-launch: collect the first application questions and turn them into FAQ and content

**Launch metrics:** qualified inquiries, sample requests, datasheet downloads, distributor orders, pipeline value, first reference installation — not signups or day-one revenue.
